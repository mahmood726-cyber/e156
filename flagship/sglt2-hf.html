SGLT2 inhibitors and heart failure

Do SGLT2 inhibitors reduce cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure across the ejection-fraction spectrum?

Type 1 · Pairwise meta-analysis · 5 randomized trials

QuestionIn adults with heart failure across the ejection-fraction spectrum, do SGLT2 inhibitors reduce the composite of cardiovascular death or worsening heart failure compared with placebo?

DatasetFive randomized, placebo-controlled outcome trials enrolling 21,947 participants — DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER and SOLOIST-WHF — reported between 2019 and 2022.

MethodTrial hazard ratios were pooled on the log scale by inverse-variance random-effects meta-analysis (DerSimonian–Laird), with a prediction interval for the effect in a new setting.

Primary resultThe pooled hazard ratio is 0.77 (95% CI 0.72 to 0.82) — about a 23% relative reduction.

RobustnessHeterogeneity is low (I² near 0%), leave-one-out keeps the estimate close, and the prediction interval still excludes “no effect”.

InterpretationSGLT2 inhibition is consistently associated with fewer heart-failure events across reduced and preserved ejection fraction, supporting a class effect.

BoundaryTrials differed in exact endpoints and populations, follow-up was roughly two years, and these pooled estimates do not establish effects on each individual component or beyond the studied horizon.

Why a prediction interval, not just a confidence interval

The confidence interval says where the average true effect lies. The prediction interval (dashed) says where the effect would plausibly fall in a new trial or setting — the honest question a clinician actually asks. When both exclude 1.0, the signal is robust.
