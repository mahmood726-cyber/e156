## Abstract

**Background.** Three PrEP formulations are used across sub-Saharan Africa: oral TDF/FTC,
the dapivirine ring, and long-acting injectable cabotegravir (CAB-LA). Efficacy and
real-world effectiveness diverge, and the divergence is not uniform across populations.

**Methods.** We synthesised three landmark randomised trials, recomputing incidence-rate
ratios (IRR) from the published person-time rates and contrasting the CAB-LA advantage over
oral PrEP by sex. Verified inputs; deterministic script.

**Results.** Oral TDF/FTC versus placebo (iPrEx, men who have sex with men) reduced HIV
incidence by **44%** (36 vs 64 infections). Against *active* oral TDF/FTC, CAB-LA was
superior in both sexes but by very different margins: in men and transgender women
(HPTN 083) IRR **0.34** (0.41 vs 1.22/100 person-years; **66% fewer** infections), and in
women (HPTN 084) IRR **0.11** (0.2 vs 1.85/100 person-years; **89% fewer**; HR 0.12,
p<0.0001). The CAB-LA advantage over oral PrEP is thus **~3× larger in women than in men**
(women/men IRR ratio 0.32). The mechanism is adherence: in HPTN 084 only **42.1%** of oral-
arm plasma samples had tenofovir consistent with daily use, versus **93%** CAB injection
coverage.

**Conclusion.** The efficacy question — does PrEP work if taken? — is settled for every
formulation. The binding constraint is **effectiveness as delivered**, which is an
adherence problem, and long-acting CAB-LA delivers its largest benefit precisely in the
population (women) where daily oral adherence is hardest. PrEP programming should
prioritise long-acting options where oral adherence is lowest.

## 1. Introduction

Pre-exposure prophylaxis prevents HIV acquisition — if the drug is present in the body at
the moment of exposure. That conditional is the entire story of PrEP implementation. Oral
TDF/FTC is highly efficacious in adherent users, yet several trials in African women (VOICE,
FEM-PrEP) failed to show benefit — not because the drug does not work, but because daily
pill-taking collapsed under real-world barriers (stigma, partner dynamics, pill fatigue).
Long-acting injectable cabotegravir, dosed every eight weeks, removes the daily-adherence
requirement. The clinical question has therefore shifted from *efficacy* to *effectiveness
as delivered*, and the policy question to *which formulation, for whom*. This paper answers
both from the verified randomised evidence and surfaces the sex-differential that should
drive programming.

## 2. Methods

We synthesised three landmark trials: iPrEx (oral TDF/FTC vs placebo, MSM), HPTN 083
(CAB-LA vs active oral TDF/FTC, cisgender men and transgender women), and HPTN 084 (CAB-LA
vs active oral TDF/FTC, women, seven sub-Saharan African countries). From each we extracted
the published person-time incidence and recomputed the incidence-rate ratio (IRR = rate_CAB
/ rate_oral), comparing it with the published hazard ratio, and contrasted the CAB-LA
advantage between the male and female trials. Adherence was read from HPTN 084's plasma
tenofovir sub-study. All values are emitted by `96-prep-verify.py`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Verified formulation effects

| Trial (PMID) | Comparison | Population | Result | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| iPrEx (21091279) | oral TDF/FTC vs **placebo** | MSM | 36 vs 64 infections | **44% ↓ incidence** |
| HPTN 083 (34379922) | **CAB-LA vs oral** TDF/FTC | men + transgender women | 0.41 vs 1.22 /100 PY | **IRR 0.34** (66% ↓ vs oral) |
| HPTN 084 (35378077) | **CAB-LA vs oral** TDF/FTC | women (7 SSA countries) | 0.20 vs 1.85 /100 PY | **IRR 0.11** (89% ↓ vs oral); HR 0.12, p<0.0001 |

The recomputed IRRs (0.336, 0.108) reproduce the published hazard ratios (0.34, 0.12) — the
CAB-LA superiority is verified from the person-time rates, not merely quoted.

### 3.2 The sex-differential — the key new finding

```
CAB-LA vs oral TDF/FTC — % fewer HIV infections than oral PrEP
Men + transgender women (HPTN 083)  ██████████████        66%
Women (HPTN 084)                     ███████████████████   89%
```

CAB-LA is superior to oral PrEP in both sexes, but the **margin of superiority is ~3× larger
in women** (women/men IRR ratio 0.32). This is not a pharmacological difference — cabotegravir
is the same drug — but a *delivery* difference: oral PrEP performs worse in women, so replacing
it with a long-acting agent recovers more prevented infections there.

### 3.3 The mechanism is adherence, not drug potency

HPTN 084 measured it directly: only **42.1%** of oral-arm plasma samples had tenofovir
concentrations consistent with daily use, against **93%** CAB injection coverage. The
oral-arm incidence of 1.85/100 person-years is an *adherence-limited* rate, not the rate a
perfectly-adherent user would experience. CAB-LA's 89% advantage in women is therefore
largely the adherence gap made visible: the injectable converts the drug's intrinsic
efficacy into realised effectiveness by removing the daily-dosing failure point. This is the
resolution of the old paradox in which oral PrEP "failed" in African women — it was never a
drug failure; it was an adherence failure that a long-acting formulation circumvents.

## 4. Discussion

The PrEP evidence base has passed the efficacy stage: every formulation works when the drug
is present at exposure, and the two head-to-head trials (HPTN 083, 084) establish that
long-acting CAB-LA is superior to daily oral TDF/FTC in both men and women. The finding that
reframes programming is the *size* of that superiority and its distribution: CAB-LA prevents
66% more infections than oral PrEP in men and transgender women, but 89% more in women — a
roughly three-fold larger advantage in exactly the population where oral adherence is lowest.
Because cabotegravir is the same molecule in both trials, this gradient cannot be
pharmacological; it is the adherence gap, quantified by HPTN 084's finding that fewer than
half of oral-arm women had drug levels consistent with daily use.

The implications are concrete and, in places, counter to how PrEP budgets are often set.
First, effectiveness — not efficacy — is the correct decision metric, and it is
population-specific: the same oral drug that performs adequately in adherent men underperforms
in women facing structural barriers to daily dosing. Second, long-acting formulations should
be prioritised precisely where oral adherence is lowest, because that is where they recover
the most prevented infections; targeting CAB-LA to the highest-adherence populations (often
the default when a product is scarce or expensive) inverts the value logic. Third, the old
narrative that "PrEP doesn't work in African women" must be retired: it was an artifact of an
adherence-limited delivery mode, and HPTN 084's 89% advantage for a long-acting agent is the
direct refutation.

None of this diminishes oral PrEP, which remains highly effective for adherent users and is
cheaper and already scaled. The point is allocative: match the formulation to the adherence
context, and reserve the largest long-acting investment for the populations and settings where
daily dosing fails — which the data identify as women in high-incidence sub-Saharan settings.

## 5. Limitations

The male/female CAB-LA contrast comes from two separate trials (HPTN 083, 084), not a single
randomised sex comparison, so it is a cross-trial inference (populations and incidence
differ). IRRs are computed from published aggregate person-time rates, not individual data.
The dapivirine ring and newer agents (lenacapavir) are not analysed here; the ring's modest
efficacy and the emerging six-monthly lenacapavir data would extend the effectiveness-by-
adherence framework. Cost, delivery infrastructure, and injection-site-reaction acceptability
(38% in HPTN 084, without discontinuation) are real implementation factors not modelled.

## 6. Conclusion

PrEP efficacy is settled; effectiveness is an adherence problem. Long-acting CAB-LA is
superior to daily oral TDF/FTC in both sexes (IRR 0.34 in men/TGW, 0.11 in women), and its
advantage is ~3× larger in women — the population where oral adherence is hardest and where
only 42% of oral users had drug levels consistent with daily use. Programmes should prioritise
long-acting PrEP where oral adherence is lowest, which is where it prevents the most
infections.
