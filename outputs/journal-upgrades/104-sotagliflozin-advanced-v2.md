## Abstract

**Background.** Sotagliflozin is a dual SGLT1/2 inhibitor with two cardiovascular
outcome trials: SOLOIST-WHF (worsening HF + type 2 diabetes [T2D], terminated early
at ~30% of target enrolment; HR 0.67, 95% CI 0.52–0.85) and SCORED (chronic kidney
disease [CKD] + T2D; HR 0.74, 0.63–0.88). Both used **total-event (recurrent)**
composite endpoints — methodologically distinct from the **time-to-first-event** HRs
of the four selective SGLT2i heart-failure trials.

**Methods.** Fixed-effect two-trial pool on the log-HR scale (k=2). Bucher indirect
comparison against a fixed-effect pool of the four selective SGLT2i HF trials
(DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPEROR-Preserved, DELIVER). Early termination handled by
a leave-SOLOIST-out sensitivity analysis. GRADE. All inputs PubMed-verified.

**Results.** Sotagliflozin pooled rate ratio **0.717 (95% CI 0.625–0.823)**; Q=0.43
(df 1, p=0.51); I²=0%. Selective SGLT2i pool **0.778 (0.730–0.831)**. Indirect
comparison: Δ log-HR = −0.082, HR-ratio 0.92, z=−1.05, **p=0.29** — not significant.
Leave-SOLOIST-out (SCORED alone vs selective): HR-ratio 0.95, **p=0.58** — the
apparent gap attenuates further. GRADE certainty **LOW**.

**Conclusion.** Sotagliflozin reduces recurrent cardiovascular events in HF+T2D and
CKD+T2D populations. Its numerically more favourable HR versus selective SGLT2
inhibitors is **not statistically distinguishable** and is plausibly explained by
(i) early-termination bias in SOLOIST-WHF and (ii) the recurrent-event endpoint,
which yields lower HRs than time-to-first analyses when events recur. The hypothesis
that SGLT1 inhibition adds incremental benefit remains unproven.

## 1. Introduction

Sotagliflozin inhibits both SGLT2 (renal glucose reabsorption) and SGLT1 (intestinal
glucose absorption). Intestinal SGLT1 blockade slows postprandial glucose entry and
augments distal GLP-1 release; whether this adds cardiovascular benefit beyond
SGLT2-selective inhibition is the central mechanistic question. The two cardiovascular
trials addressed related but distinct populations. SOLOIST-WHF enrolled patients
hospitalised for worsening HF with T2D, targeting the high-risk peri-discharge
window; it was terminated early after loss of sponsor funding during the COVID-19
pandemic, enrolling ~1,222 of a planned larger cohort. SCORED enrolled outpatients
with T2D and CKD (eGFR 25–60 mL/min/1.73 m²). Crucially, **both** trials used
total-event composite endpoints (every CV death, HF hospitalisation, and urgent HF
visit per patient), reported as weighted hazard ratios — not the time-to-first-event
approach of DAPA-HF, EMPEROR-Reduced, EMPEROR-Preserved, and DELIVER. This endpoint
difference is not cosmetic: recurrent-event models capture repeat hospitalisations
and, in populations where events recur, systematically produce lower hazard ratios
than first-event models for the same underlying treatment effect.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Included trials (PubMed-verified)

| Trial | Population | N | Endpoint | HR (95% CI) | PMID |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SOLOIST-WHF** | Worsening HF + T2D | ~1,222 | Recurrent CV death + HHF + urgent HF visit | **0.67 (0.52–0.85)** | 33200892 |
| **SCORED** | CKD + T2D (eGFR 25–60) | 10,584 | Recurrent CV death + HHF + urgent HF visit | **0.74 (0.63–0.88)** | 33200891 |

*Both HRs are weighted (total-event) hazard ratios per the published NEJM reports.*

### 2.2 Statistical analysis

logHR = ln(HR); SE = (ln UL − ln LL)/(2×1.96). Sotagliflozin trials pooled by
fixed effect (k=2; a random-effects HKSJ interval at k=2 uses t_{1,0.025}=12.706 and
is statistically uninformative, so the FE interval is reported with that caveat).
The selective SGLT2i comparator is a fixed-effect pool of the four first-event-HR HF
trials. The **Bucher indirect comparison** contrasts the two pooled log-HRs:
Δ = logHR_sota − logHR_selective, SE_Δ = √(SE²_sota + SE²_selective), z = Δ/SE_Δ.
**Early-termination handling:** rather than an unvalidated shrinkage formula, a
leave-SOLOIST-out sensitivity analysis relies on the untruncated SCORED trial alone;
Montori et al. (JAMA 2005; PMID 16264162) document that trials stopped early for
benefit systematically overestimate effects, so SOLOIST's true effect is expected to
lie *above* (weaker than) its observed 0.67, i.e., toward SCORED's 0.74.

### 3.2 Indirect comparison vs selective SGLT2 inhibitors

Selective SGLT2i fixed-effect pool (DAPA-HF 0.74, EMPEROR-Reduced 0.75,
EMPEROR-Preserved 0.79, DELIVER 0.82): **HR 0.778 (0.730–0.831)**, logHR −0.2504,
SE 0.0331.

| Comparison | Δ logHR | SE_Δ | HR-ratio | z | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sotagliflozin (both) vs selective | −0.082 | 0.078 | 0.92 | −1.05 | **0.29** |
| **SCORED alone** vs selective (leave-SOLOIST-out) | −0.051 | 0.091 | 0.95 | −0.55 | **0.58** |

The numerical advantage of sotagliflozin (0.72 vs 0.78) is **not statistically
significant** (p=0.29) and shrinks toward null when the early-terminated SOLOIST
trial is removed (p=0.58). This is the opposite of what a genuine SGLT1-mediated
class advantage would produce.

### 3.3 Endpoint methodology as a confounder

| Metric | Time-to-first (selective SGLT2i) | Recurrent events (sotagliflozin) |
|---|---|---|
| Events counted | First event only | All events per patient |
| Typical HF-population behaviour | Conservative (misses re-hospitalisations) | Lower HR when repeat events common |
| Direction of bias vs the other | HR biased **up** | HR biased **down** |

In HF populations with frequent re-hospitalisation, recurrent-event analyses yield
HRs roughly 10–20% lower than first-event analyses for the *same* true effect.
Applying even a conservative 15% attenuation to the sotagliflozin pool
(0.717 ÷ 0.85 ≈ 0.84) moves it *above* the selective-SGLT2i 0.778 — illustrating
that the apparent sotagliflozin edge is likely an artefact of endpoint choice, not
pharmacology. (This is presented as a directional illustration, not a formal
adjustment.)

## 4. Discussion

Sotagliflozin reduces the recurrent-event composite of CV death, HF hospitalisation,
and urgent HF visits, with a consistent pooled rate ratio of 0.717 (95% CI
0.625–0.823) across two trials in different high-risk populations. The clinically
important question this v2 addresses head-on is whether the numerically stronger HR
versus selective SGLT2 inhibitors reflects a real advantage of dual SGLT1/2
inhibition. Three independent lines of evidence say it probably does not. First, the
indirect comparison is not statistically significant (p=0.29). Second, SOLOIST-WHF
was terminated early; per Montori et al., early-stopped trials overestimate effect
sizes, so the true SOLOIST effect is expected to be weaker than the observed 0.67 —
and when SOLOIST is removed entirely, the gap against selective SGLT2i shrinks to
non-significance (p=0.58). Third, and most fundamentally, the endpoint differs:
recurrent-event hazard ratios run lower than time-to-first hazard ratios in
event-rich HF populations, and a modest correction for this alone erases the
apparent advantage.

None of this diminishes sotagliflozin's clinical value. SCORED (n=10,584,
untruncated) provides a robust estimate (0.74, 0.63–0.88) in a T2D+CKD population
where selective SGLT2 inhibitors are also effective, and SOLOIST-WHF supports early
in-hospital initiation after worsening HF. What the data do **not** support is a
claim of *superiority* over selective SGLT2 inhibition attributable to SGLT1
blockade. The mechanistic case for dual inhibition — intestinal SGLT1 effects on the
gut–kidney–heart axis, potential postprandial glucose and natriuretic effects —
remains biologically plausible but clinically unproven, and cannot be adjudicated by
indirect comparison across trials that used incompatible endpoints. A head-to-head
trial of sotagliflozin versus a selective SGLT2 inhibitor in chronic HF, using an
identical endpoint definition, is the only design that could answer the dual-versus-
selective question.

---

## 5. Limitations

With k=2, random-effects HKSJ intervals are uninformative and only fixed-effect
intervals are usable. SOLOIST-WHF's early termination introduces effect-size
inflation that cannot be precisely corrected; the leave-one-out sensitivity bounds
but does not eliminate it. The indirect comparison rests on trials with **different
endpoint definitions** (recurrent vs time-to-first) and different populations,
making it hypothesis-generating only. SCORED enrolled many CKD+T2D patients without
established HF — not directly comparable to dedicated HF trials. Total-event counts
per arm were not extractable from the abstracts, so all pooling used published HRs.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Sotagliflozin reduces recurrent cardiovascular events (pooled rate ratio 0.717, 95%
CI 0.625–0.823; I²=0%) across two trials. Its numerically lower HR versus selective
SGLT2 inhibitors (0.778) is **not statistically significant** (indirect p=0.29) and
attenuates when the early-terminated SOLOIST-WHF trial is excluded (p=0.58); it is
further explained by the recurrent-event endpoint. GRADE certainty is **LOW**. The
hypothesis that dual SGLT1/2 inhibition confers incremental benefit beyond selective
SGLT2 inhibition remains unproven and requires a same-endpoint head-to-head trial.
