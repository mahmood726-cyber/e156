## Abstract

**Background.** Angiography cannot resolve stent under-expansion, incomplete lesion
coverage, or edge dissection. Intravascular imaging (IVI) — intravascular ultrasound
(IVUS) or optical coherence tomography (OCT) — provides cross-sectional guidance.

**Methods.** Four RCTs (N=6,536) comparing IVI- versus angiography-guided PCI with a
clinical composite endpoint were pooled on the log scale by REML random-effects with
Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman (HKSJ) confidence intervals (t_{k−1}, q≥1 floor),
Cochran Q/I², a 95% prediction interval, leave-one-out, IVUS-vs-OCT subgroups, and a
descriptive small-study check (correlation of log-effect with standard error). All
computations are reproduced by `29-ivi-pci-verify.py`.

**Results.** Pooled **HR 0.702 (HKSJ 95% CI 0.480–1.028)**; I²=39.7%; τ²=0.021;
Q=4.97 (df 3, p=0.17); 95% PI 0.389–1.267. The CI marginally crosses unity, driven
by the large, null ILUMIEN IV trial (HR 0.90). Leave-one-out pooled HR ranges
0.633–0.751 (directional stability). The **correlation between log-HR and standard
error is −0.94**: smaller, less-precise trials report larger benefits, and the single
largest trial is null — a textbook small-study-effects pattern that, although
formally underpowered at k=4, cautions against taking the point estimate at face
value. IVUS subgroup point HR 0.59, OCT 0.81 (both k=2; intervals uninformative).

**Conclusion.** IVI-guided PCI is associated with a lower composite event rate, but
the benefit is imprecise, heterogeneous, and concentrated in smaller trials. GRADE
certainty is MODERATE. IVI is best reserved for complex lesions (bifurcation, long,
calcified, left main), where the individual trials show significant benefit and
mechanistic plausibility is greatest.

## 1. Introduction

Angiography renders only a luminal silhouette: it cannot quantify plaque burden,
calcification depth, true reference-vessel dimensions, or post-deployment stent
apposition. Under-expansion — present in a large fraction of angiographically
"successful" cases — predicts stent thrombosis, restenosis, and major adverse
cardiovascular events (MACE). IVUS measures external-elastic-lamina reference and
plaque burden; OCT provides ~10–15 µm axial resolution for apposition, dissection,
and expansion at the cost of contrast flushing. Landmark 2023 trials (OCTOBER,
ILUMIEN IV) were the first adequately powered head-to-head comparisons. This v2
synthesis reproduces the pooled estimate deterministically and adds a small-study
sensitivity analysis, because the credibility of a k=4 pooled benefit depends on
whether its magnitude is an artefact of trial size.

## 2. Methods

Four RCTs met inclusion (individual randomisation to IVI vs angiography; composite of
cardiac/CV death, target-vessel/lesion MI, ischaemia-driven revascularisation;
≥12-month follow-up). For trials reporting a hazard ratio, logHR and SE were derived
from the published HR and 95% CI. For **ULTIMATE-3yr** (event counts, no abstract
HR), a log risk ratio was computed from 47/714 versus 76/734 with the large-sample
variance. Pooling used REML random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird avoided at small *k*),
Paule–Mandel sensitivity, HKSJ intervals with t_{k−1} and a q≥1 floor, Cochran Q and
I² from fixed-effect weights, and a t_{k−1} prediction interval. Leave-one-out and
device-class subgroups were pre-specified. A **descriptive** small-study check — the
Pearson correlation between logHR and SE — is reported with the explicit caveat that
k=4 is far below the k≥10 threshold at which Egger/Begg tests are valid.

### 3.2 Pooled estimate and heterogeneity

REML τ²=0.021; Q=4.97 (df 3, p=0.17); **I²=39.7%** (moderate). Pooled **HR 0.702
(HKSJ 95% CI 0.480–1.028)**; the interval marginally crosses unity. The 95%
prediction interval (**0.389–1.267**) spans harm: a future trial in a lower-complexity
or lower-adherence setting could show no benefit. Paule–Mandel sensitivity (τ²=0.023)
gives HR 0.701 (0.478–1.027) — unchanged.

### 3.3 Leave-one-out

| Omitted | Pooled HR | HKSJ 95% CI | I² |
|---|---|---|---|
| IVUS-XPL | 0.751 | 0.468–1.204 | 22.0% |
| ULTIMATE-3yr | 0.715 | 0.346–1.478 | 53.5% |
| OCTOBER | 0.686 | 0.321–1.462 | 59.4% |
| ILUMIEN IV | 0.633 | 0.389–1.028 | 0.0% |

The pooled HR is directionally stable (0.633–0.751). Removing **ILUMIEN IV**
eliminates heterogeneity (I²→0%) and lowers the pooled HR to 0.633 — the clearest
evidence that this single large, null trial is both the heterogeneity source and the
main brake on the pooled benefit.

### 3.4 Small-study-effects sensitivity analysis (new in v2)

Across the four trials the correlation between the log hazard ratio and its standard
error is **−0.94**. Because a *more negative* logHR is a *larger* benefit, this means
the **less precise (smaller) trials report the larger effects**, and the single most
precise trial (ILUMIEN IV) is null. This is the canonical small-study-effects
signature — the same pattern that inflates naïve pooled estimates through publication
bias, outcome-reporting bias, or genuine trial-size heterogeneity (e.g., smaller
single-country trials enrolling higher-complexity lesions or applying imaging
findings more aggressively). **At k=4 this is descriptive only** — formal Egger/Begg
tests require k≥10 and would be uninterpretable here — but the direction and strength
are strong enough to reinforce cautious interpretation. The reading is *not* that the
true effect equals the small trials' large benefit; rather, that the pooled HR (~0.70)
may itself be optimistic relative to what a large, adherence-rigorous trial would
show, since the one such trial available (ILUMIEN IV) was null. The pooled estimate
and that null anchor bracket the plausible truth, and neither supports a strong
universal-adoption claim.

### 3.5 Device-class subgroups (k=2 each — point estimates only)

IVUS subgroup (IVUS-XPL + ULTIMATE-3yr): point HR **0.586**, I²=0%. OCT subgroup
(OCTOBER + ILUMIEN IV): point HR **0.806**, I²=19%. Both HKSJ intervals (t_{1}=12.706)
span ~0.09–4 and are **statistically uninformative**; the apparent IVUS>OCT ordering
is confounded by lesion complexity (OCTOBER bifurcation-only; ILUMIEN IV broad) and
follow-up. Head-to-head OCTIVUS data show OCT non-inferior to IVUS for clinical
outcomes, so modality choice can follow availability and operator familiarity.

### 3.6 GRADE

| Outcome | Trials | Estimate | Certainty | Downgrades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Composite MACE/TVF | 4 RCTs | HR 0.70 (0.48–1.03) | **MODERATE** | Imprecision (CI crosses 1); small-study signal |

No downgrade for inconsistency (I²=40%, p_Q=0.17, all trials directionally favour
IVI, direction preserved in every leave-one-out). Open-label design is intrinsic to
the intervention.

## 4. Discussion

Four RCTs and 6,536 patients yield a pooled HR of 0.702 for IVI- versus
angiography-guided PCI, with a HKSJ interval that marginally crosses unity and a
prediction interval spanning harm. The result is best read as *probable modest
benefit with material uncertainty*, not as an established universal effect. Three
features drive the caution. First, **ILUMIEN IV** — the largest trial — was null for
TVF (HR 0.90, p=0.45) despite achieving its anatomical endpoint (larger minimum stent
area), and it both dominates the pooled weight and generates the heterogeneity.
Second, the **small-study-effects correlation (−0.94)** shows the benefit is
concentrated in the smaller trials, exactly the configuration that warrants
discounting. Third, endpoint definitions differ (target-lesion MACE vs target-vessel
TVF), mixing component scopes on the log scale.

Yet the clinical signal is not empty. Three of four trials individually reach
significance (IVUS-XPL 0.48, ULTIMATE-3yr 0.64, OCTOBER 0.70), and the mechanistic
case is strongest exactly where they enrolled: **complex lesions** — long segments
(IVUS-XPL), all-comer real-world stenting (ULTIMATE), and bifurcations (OCTOBER) —
where imaging changes operator decisions (reference sizing, expansion, side-branch
coverage). The pragmatic synthesis is therefore lesion-selective: IVI as standard of
care for complex/high-risk PCI, discretionary for simple lesions where the number
needed to treat rises steeply and the null ILUMIEN-IV-type result is most
representative.

## 5. Limitations

k=4 keeps HKSJ intervals wide and makes small-study testing descriptive only.
ILUMIEN IV dominates the weight. ULTIMATE contributes a log risk ratio (no abstract
HR), which approximates logHR only under proportional hazards. Endpoint scope differs
across trials. All trials are open-label. Individual-patient data (SYNTAX score,
calcification, vessel size, diabetes) — needed to identify who benefits — are
unavailable; a formal IPD meta-regression is warranted.

## 6. Conclusion

IVI-guided PCI is associated with a pooled HR of 0.702 (HKSJ 95% CI 0.480–1.028;
I²=39.7%; PI 0.389–1.267) for the composite endpoint. The benefit is imprecise,
concentrated in smaller trials (log-effect/SE correlation −0.94), and offset by a
null result in the largest trial. GRADE certainty is MODERATE. IVI should be standard
for complex, high-risk lesions and individualised for simple ones; both IVUS and OCT
are acceptable. A large, adherence-rigorous trial and IPD meta-regression are the
priorities.
