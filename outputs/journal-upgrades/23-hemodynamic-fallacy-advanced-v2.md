## Abstract

**Background.** Mechanical circulatory support (MCS) reliably improves haemodynamics
in cardiogenic shock (CS), yet whether it improves survival has been contested for two
decades. The apparent conflict dissolves once trials are stratified by device and
comparator.

**Methods.** Systematic synthesis of randomised MCS trials in CS reporting all-cause
mortality. Effects pooled on the log scale (REML random-effects, HKSJ t_{k−1}
intervals with q≥1 floor, Cochran Q/I², prediction interval, leave-one-out). The
newly-added ECLS-SHOCK anchors a VA-ECMO stratum; the Zeymer IPD-MA is the external
benchmark. All inputs PubMed-verified.

**Results.** A device-vs-standard/no-support pool (IABP-SHOCK II, DanGer Shock,
ECLS-SHOCK; k=3) gives **0.92 (HKSJ 95% CI 0.67–1.27)**, I²=25%. Removing the single
positive trial (DanGer) yields **0.97 (0.39–2.40)** — a flat null. Stratified: **IABP**
null (RR 0.96, 0.79–1.17); **VA-ECMO** null (ECLS-SHOCK RR 0.98, 0.80–1.19; Zeymer
IPD-MA OR 0.93, 0.66–1.29) with doubled bleeding (OR 2.44) and tripled vascular
complications (OR 3.53); **Impella vs IABP** null (IMPRESS HR 0.96, 0.42–2.18). The
**only** positive mortality signal is **DanGer Shock** (Impella CP vs standard care,
STEMI-only, 180-day: HR **0.74, 0.55–0.99**), achieved with a fourfold safety-event
excess (composite RR 4.74) and more renal-replacement therapy (RR 1.98).

**Conclusion.** "MCS improves haemodynamics, therefore MCS improves survival" is false
as a general claim. Haemodynamic augmentation is necessary but not sufficient. The lone
survival benefit is narrowly specific — a particular device (microaxial pump), in a
particular phenotype (STEMI-CS), against a particular comparator (standard care) — and
is bought with substantial device-related morbidity.

## 1. Introduction

Cardiogenic shock — inadequate tissue perfusion from cardiac failure — retains a
30-day mortality of 40–50% in acute-MI CS despite primary PCI. The haemodynamic
rationale for MCS is mechanistically seductive: if the lesion is lost forward stroke
volume, restore cardiac index and unload the ventricle, and the ischaemia–hypoperfusion
spiral should break. Registries endorsed this logic. Randomised trials, repeatedly,
have not — devices that move Swan-Ganz numbers have generally not moved the mortality
curve. This v2 resolves the apparent paradox not by re-litigating a single pooled
estimate but by stratifying the randomised evidence by **device and comparator**, and
by adding the two pieces of 2023 evidence that settle the VA-ECMO question:
ECLS-SHOCK and the Zeymer IPD meta-analysis.

## 2. Methods

Randomised trials of any MCS device (IABP, microaxial flow pump, VA-ECMO) versus
control or lesser support in adult CS, reporting all-cause mortality at ≥30 days, were
eligible. For each trial, logHR (or logRR where only an RR was reported) and its SE
were derived from the published estimate and 95% CI; RR↔HR equivalence is reasonable
for short-horizon all-cause mortality and is declared as a limitation. Pooling used
REML random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird avoided at small *k*), Paule–Mandel sensitivity,
HKSJ intervals with t_{k−1} and a q≥1 floor, Cochran Q and I², a t_{k−1} prediction
interval, and leave-one-out. Trials were **stratified by device and comparator** rather
than force-pooled: IMPRESS (Impella vs IABP) is a device-vs-device contrast and is not
combined with device-vs-standard trials. The Zeymer 2023 VA-ECMO IPD-MA is cited as an
external benchmark, not recomputed. Numbers are emitted by
`23-cardiogenic-shock-verify.py`.

### 3.3 VA-ECMO stratum — now quantitatively settled

ECLS-SHOCK (the largest single trial) shows RR 0.98 (0.80–1.19; p=0.81). The Zeymer
2023 individual-patient-data meta-analysis of **four** VA-ECMO trials (n=567; 284 ECMO,
283 control) reports 30-day death **OR 0.93 (0.66–1.29)** — not significant — with
**major bleeding OR 2.44 (1.55–3.84)** and **peripheral vascular complications OR 3.53
(1.70–7.34)**, and consistent null subgroups (all p≥0.079). The routine early use of
VA-ECMO in infarct-related CS does not reduce mortality and increases harm.

### 3.4 The haemodynamic–mortality dissociation (mechanistic anchor)

The Thiele 2017 collaborative meta-analysis of active MCS (Impella/TandemHeart) vs IABP
remains the cleanest demonstration: mean arterial pressure rose +11.85 mmHg (p=0.02)
and lactate fell −1.36 mmol/L (p=0.02), yet 30-day mortality was numerically identical
(**RR 1.01**, I²=0%). Improving the catheter numbers did not improve survival. The
mechanisms are well described: (i) **microvascular no-reflow** — epicardial and systemic
flow restoration does not guarantee myocardial reperfusion; (ii) **systemic inflammation**
— the TNF-α/IL-6/complement cascade driving vasoplegia and multi-organ failure is
flow-independent; (iii) **time-to-reperfusion displacement** — device insertion can
delay the intervention (revascularisation) that actually determines salvage; and (iv)
**dynamic cardiac power output**, not admission haemodynamics, is the validated survival
correlate (CPO >0.6 W at 24 h). Static haemodynamic snapshots are not validated
surrogates for survival in CS.

## 4. Discussion

Stratifying by device and comparator dissolves the "haemodynamic fallacy" paradox.
Three of the four device-vs-standard contrasts are null: IABP (IABP-SHOCK II), VA-ECMO
(ECLS-SHOCK plus the Zeymer IPD-MA of four trials), and Impella-vs-IABP (IMPRESS). The
null-device trials are so concordant that pooling them gives I²=0% at HR 0.97. Only
**DanGer Shock** is positive — and its result is not a vindication of haemodynamic
surrogates but a precise identification of the conditions under which unloading pays
off: a **microaxial pump**, in **STEMI-CS specifically** (viable perimeter myocardium,
large territory at risk), against **standard care**, in a protocolised, high-volume,
shock-team setting, measured at **180 days**. Even there, the mortality benefit
(HR 0.74) is bought with a fourfold excess of the composite safety endpoint (RR 4.74),
more bleeding and limb ischaemia, and a near-doubling of renal-replacement therapy
(RR 1.98). The net clinical benefit is real but conditional, and it does not generalise
to unselected CS or to VA-ECMO.

This reframing has direct guideline implications. The routine, haemodynamically-driven
escalation of MCS in unselected CS is not supported. VA-ECMO, specifically, should not
be deployed for infarct-related CS on the expectation of a survival benefit — the two
2023 pieces of evidence (ECLS-SHOCK, Zeymer) close that question in the negative while
documenting substantial harm. The forward path is phenotype-directed: SCAI stage
trajectory and 24-hour cardiac power output, matched to the one device–population
pairing (microaxial pump in STEMI-CS) with randomised support, embedded in
multidisciplinary shock-team infrastructure that is itself an active co-intervention.

## 5. Limitations

Small *k* within strata; the VA-ECMO independent pool relies on ECLS-SHOCK plus the
published Zeymer IPD-MA rather than a recomputed two-trial pool (ECMO-CS all-cause
mortality is a secondary of a composite-primary trial). Mixed effect measures (RR vs
HR). ECMO-CS had substantial control-arm crossover to downstream ECMO, biasing its
contrast toward null. DanGer Shock's p=0.04 at 180 days in a long-enrolment trial
warrants replication. Haemodynamic sub-study data (per-arm CI/PCWP) from DanGer and
IMPRESS require full-text access and are not used here.

## 6. Conclusion

Across randomised trials, the general proposition that MCS lowers mortality in
cardiogenic shock is not supported: IABP, VA-ECMO, and Impella-vs-IABP are each null,
and pooling the null-device trials gives HR 0.97 (I²=0%). The addition of ECLS-SHOCK
and the Zeymer IPD-MA settles VA-ECMO in the negative — no survival benefit, doubled
bleeding, tripled vascular complications. The single positive result (DanGer Shock,
HR 0.74) is device-, phenotype-, and comparator-specific and carries a fourfold safety
cost. Haemodynamic improvement remains necessary but not sufficient for survival; the
therapeutic question is no longer whether MCS augments output, but whether a specific
device–patient–comparator triad can translate that augmentation into life saved.
