## Abstract

**Background:** Severe symptomatic tricuspid regurgitation (TR) is common in
elderly, comorbid patients and historically undertreated because of prohibitive
surgical risk. Transcatheter tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (TEER) offers a
lower-risk option; we summarise what the randomised evidence actually establishes.

**Methods:** Focused synthesis anchored on the only pivotal randomised trial,
TRILUMINATE Pivotal (TriClip TEER vs medical therapy) [1], with 2-year durability
from the single-arm TRILUMINATE cohort [2]. All figures verified against the
primary publications; the KCCQ between-group difference recomputed from reported
means/standard errors.

**Results:** In TRILUMINATE Pivotal (N=350; mean age 78; 54.9% women), the
hierarchical primary composite (death or tricuspid-valve surgery; heart-failure
hospitalisation; ≥15-point KCCQ improvement) favoured TEER with a **win ratio of
1.48 (95% CI 1.06–2.13; P=0.02)**. The benefit was driven by quality of life:
**KCCQ change +12.3±1.8 (TEER) vs +0.6±1.8 (control)** points (between-group
difference ≈ 11.7 points; recomputed z ≈ 4.6, P<0.001). **TR was reduced to ≤
moderate at 30 days in 87.0% vs 4.8%** (P<0.001). Death or tricuspid-valve
surgery and heart-failure hospitalisation **did not differ** between groups.
TEER was safe (98.3% free from major adverse events at 30 days). At 2 years,
TR reduction was sustained in 75% of patients [2].

**Conclusion:** The randomised evidence for tricuspid TEER shows a safe procedure
that durably reduces TR and improves quality of life, with **no demonstrated
reduction in mortality or heart-failure hospitalisation** at the trial horizon.
GRADE: MODERATE certainty for the QoL-driven composite; LOW for mortality
(single trial, imprecise, no signal).

## 1. Background

TR is a progressive disease of the elderly, frequently secondary to
atrial fibrillation or left-heart disease, and historically managed by
"watchful waiting" because isolated surgical tricuspid repair carries high
operative risk. Transcatheter options — coaptation/edge-to-edge devices
(TriClip, PASCAL), annuloplasty systems, and orthotopic replacement — have
emerged as lower-risk structural alternatives. Until TRILUMINATE Pivotal, the
evidence was almost entirely single-arm registry data; the pivotal trial provides
the first randomised comparison against medical therapy.

---

## 2. Methods

Focused synthesis. The primary estimand is the TRILUMINATE Pivotal hierarchical
composite win ratio [1]; secondary estimands are KCCQ change, TR-grade reduction,
mortality, and heart-failure hospitalisation, all taken verbatim from the primary
report. Two-year durability is from the single-arm TRILUMINATE cohort [2]. The
KCCQ between-group difference was recomputed from the reported means and standard
errors (difference = 12.3 − 0.6 = 11.7 points; SE = √(1.8² + 1.8²) = 2.55;
z = 11.7/2.55 ≈ 4.6). Win ratios and time-to-event estimands cannot be recomputed
without individual-participant data and are reported as published.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 TRILUMINATE Pivotal — verified primary results [1]

| Endpoint | TEER | Control | Effect | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary hierarchical composite | — | — | **Win ratio 1.48 (95% CI 1.06–2.13)** | 0.02 |
| KCCQ change (points, mean±SE) | +12.3±1.8 | +0.6±1.8 | Δ ≈ 11.7 (z≈4.6) | <0.001 |
| TR ≤ moderate at 30 days | 87.0% | 4.8% | +82.2 pp | <0.001 |
| Death or tricuspid-valve surgery | — | — | no apparent difference | — |
| HF hospitalisation | — | — | no apparent difference | — |
| Freedom from major adverse events (30 d) | 98.3% | — | — | — |

N=350 (175 per group); mean age 78 years; 54.9% women. The win ratio's advantage
derives overwhelmingly from the KCCQ (quality-of-life) tier of the hierarchy;
the harder clinical tiers (death/surgery, HF hospitalisation) contributed little
and did not differ between groups.

### 3.2 Durability at 2 years (single-arm TRILUMINATE cohort) [2]

TR was reduced to moderate or less in 60% and by ≥1 grade in 85.4% of subjects,
with reduction sustained in 75% at 2 years. Two-year all-cause mortality was
18.7% and cardiovascular mortality 15.3%; all-cause hospitalisation fell from
1.30 to 0.66 events/patient-year (−49%, P<0.0001). These are single-arm data and
carry no control comparison.

## 4. Discussion

Read accurately, TRILUMINATE Pivotal supports a clear but bounded conclusion:
tricuspid TEER is **safe**, **durably reduces TR**, and **improves quality of
life**, but has **not** been shown to reduce death or heart-failure
hospitalisation within the trial's follow-up. The statistically significant
primary win ratio (1.48) is real but is driven by the KCCQ tier — a
patient-important outcome, but a softer one than mortality — so the result should
be communicated as a quality-of-life and TR-reduction benefit, not a survival
benefit. This distinction is exactly what the v1 draft's inflated win ratio (1.80)
and mis-stated KCCQ percentages obscured.

Two implications follow. First, for **shared decision-making**, patients should be
told the evidence supports symptom and QoL improvement with a safe procedure, not
a proven mortality reduction. Second, for **evidence appraisal**, the win-ratio
architecture means headline significance can rest on the lowest (QoL) tier of a
hierarchy; transparent reporting of each tier's contribution is essential.
Longer-term and larger randomised data (and replacement-device trials such as
TRISCEND II/Evoque) are needed to determine whether TR reduction translates into
hard-outcome benefit.

---

## 5. Limitations

Single randomised trial; the primary composite is QoL-weighted and open-label
(KCCQ is patient-reported, susceptible to unblinded assessment). Win ratio and
time-to-event effects are reported as published (no IPD for recomputation).
Two-year durability data are single-arm and uncontrolled. This focused v2
deliberately does **not** propagate the v1 registry meta-analysis figures
(reported I² 62–71%), which require independent re-extraction and verification
before use; only primary-source-verified numbers are presented here.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Tricuspid TEER (TriClip) in TRILUMINATE Pivotal produced a significant primary
win ratio of **1.48 (95% CI 1.06–2.13; P=0.02)**, driven by quality-of-life
improvement (KCCQ +12.3 vs +0.6 points) and marked TR reduction (≤moderate at
30 days 87.0% vs 4.8%), with no difference in death or heart-failure
hospitalisation and an excellent safety profile. It is a safe, QoL- and
TR-improving therapy of as-yet-unproven mortality benefit. The v1 draft's win
ratio (1.80), KCCQ percentages, and trial citation were incorrect and are
corrected here.
