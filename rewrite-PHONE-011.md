## Entry 518 ([529/921]) — 

<details><summary>Metadata</summary>

```
Topic: Predicting Global Trial Success: The Transportability Index and Evidence Yield Metric
Type: methods
Estimand: Transportability Index (0.0 to 1.0)
Data: AACT (n=3,600+), IHME GBD 2023, WHO GHO/GHED, WB WDI
Path: D:\Projects\trial-transportability-atlas
```

</details>

### Original (frozen — do not edit)

```
This study introduces the Transportability Index (TI), a mathematical framework for quantifying the "clinical distance" between trial origins and target regions. Using a multi-source synthesis of AACT (n=3,600+), IHME, WHO, and World Bank datasets, we analyzed regional readiness across three cardiovascular classes (HFrEF, SGLT2i, GLP-1a). We find that physician density is the primary rate-limiting step for evidence transport in Africa (TI ~0.43), where doubling workforce capacity provides 3x more transportability lift than equivalent GDP growth. Conversely, Asia shows a "middle-income convergence" pattern (TI ~0.66), where economic uplift is now the dominant lever for reaching North American parity. We further introduce the Predicted Evidence Yield (PEY) metric, which discounts local disease burden by system readiness. Despite lower TI scores than South America, Asia's massive burden (>100M DALYs) makes it the global priority for evidence transport (PEY ~79k). These metrics enable deterministic, data-driven planning for global clinical translation and health system hardening.
```
